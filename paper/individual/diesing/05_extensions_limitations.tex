\section*{Extensions and Limitations}

One limitation is that firms choose their cash reserves. Firms with more cash may also be better prepared or insured, which can independently affect investment after Helene. Firm fixed effects and observable controls do not fully separate these influences. A positive $b_2$ would therefore support an association between cash and investment, but would not establish a causal cash effect.

As an extension, I would replace cash holdings with net short-term liquidity, measured as $(\texttt{CHEQ} - \texttt{DLCQ})/\texttt{ATQ}$ at the end of 2023. \texttt{DLCQ} records debt in current liabilities. Subtracting these obligations accounts for funds potentially needed for debt repayment. I would retain negative values and use the same sample, investment outcome and regression. This would show whether the relationship persists after accounting for these obligations.
