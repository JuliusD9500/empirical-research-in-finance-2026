\section*{Expected Outcome}

My main hypothesis ($H_1$) is that the change in capital expenditure associated with exposure to Helene is more positive for firms with higher pre-storm cash holdings. Cash can finance replacement assets and maintain planned investment when operating receipts fall. It can also cover the period before external finance or insurance payments become available.

Firms with more cash may increase capital expenditure more or reduce it less, so $H_1$ does not require an overall rise in investment. Insurance and access to credit may reduce the importance of cash. Conversely, shortages of labour or equipment may delay investment even when funds are available.
