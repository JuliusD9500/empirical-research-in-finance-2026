\section*{Data}

I would use a quarterly panel of firms domiciled and listed in the United States with SIC codes 2000 to 3999 (Division D: Manufacturing). Focusing on manufacturing links the analysis to physical production assets and their replacement after storm damage. Firms would need December fiscal year ends and verifiable US production locations. The sample would cover eight quarters before Helene (2022Q3 to 2024Q2) and four quarters afterwards (2024Q4 to 2025Q3). Two years before Helene provide two annual cycles for assessing prior investment trends. The following year would capture initial investment responses as firms address replacement needs and reconsider planned projects. I would exclude 2024Q3 because the storm occurred during that quarter. The comparison would then use only quarters entirely before or after Helene.

I would obtain financial data from Compustat North America through WRDS.\footnote{Access to Compustat requires an account, which I would need to obtain before collecting the data.} Company filings available through the SEC would provide annual reports with production locations. These filings could also supply financial data if Compustat were unavailable, although the figures would need to be collected and checked individually. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) is the US agency coordinating disaster assistance. Its declarations identify counties approved for assistance after Helene.

Within Compustat, the variable \texttt{CAPXY} records capital expenditure accumulated since the beginning of the fiscal year. I would use this to calculate quarterly capital expenditure (\texttt{CAPX\_Q}) by subtracting the previous quarter's accumulated amount within the same fiscal year. The first quarter requires no subtraction because its value already covers only that quarter. I would measure investment ($I_{it}$) as 100 times quarterly expenditure divided by total assets at the end of 2023. Keeping this denominator fixed prevents the investment ratio from rising simply because reported assets fall after the storm.

Cash holdings ($C_i$) would be calculated from two Compustat items measured at the end of 2023. Cash and short-term investments are recorded as \texttt{CHEQ} and total assets as \texttt{ATQ}. Dividing the first by the second measures cash reserves relative to firm size before Helene. It avoids using cash balances already affected by the storm.

The exposure dummy ($E_i$) would equal one if at least one principal US production facility reported before Helene is located in a county approved by FEMA for household assistance after the storm. It would equal zero otherwise. This measures geographic exposure rather than confirmed damage to a firm. Firms without such facilities would form the control group, provided they operate in comparable industries and have similar characteristics before the storm. Production locations are used because headquarters may be elsewhere.

I would exclude firms with incomplete location records or overlapping facility exposure to other major storms in 2024. I would also check whether the financial figures cover the same business units over time. A fall in reported investment after selling a subsidiary should not automatically be attributed to Helene. Firms leaving the market would remain in the sample for available periods. Missing observations would not be replaced by zero.
