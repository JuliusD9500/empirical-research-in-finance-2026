\section*{Expected Outcome}

My main hypothesis (H1) is that firms directly exposed to Helene cut their R\&D intensity after September 2024 compared to unexposed firms, and at the same time hold more cash.

I expect this for two reasons. First, R\&D is hard to keep after a shock. It only pays off in the long run, it is risky, and banks do not accept it as collateral, so it is difficult to finance with debt. It is also easier to postpone than repairing a damaged building. Cash is the opposite. It can be used for anything if another disaster happens, like repairs or to cover lower sales. So managers in hit areas probably prefer to keep cash as protection instead of spending on R\&D.

This is close to what Dessaint and Matray (2017) find. They show that even firms that were only near a hurricane, not directly hit, increase cash by about one percentage point of assets. They also talk a lot more about hurricane risk in their annual reports after the shock. But the effect becomes smaller when firms have seen hurricanes before, and the extra cash is much larger than the actual expected losses. This suggests managers overreact because the risk feels very close, and investors value this extra cash less. For Helene I expect the same kind of reaction: R\&D goes down while cash goes up. If only R\&D fell, it could just mean sales were lower. But if cash rises at the same time, it points more to precaution.
