\section*{Methodology}

I would estimate a difference-in-differences model by OLS to compare changes in exposed and unexposed firms:
\begin{equation*}
  Y_{it} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t
  + \beta(E_i \times \mathrm{Post}_t) + \varepsilon_{it}.
\end{equation*}
Here, $i$ identifies firms and $t$ identifies quarters. I would run the regression separately for R\&D investment and cash holdings, as defined above. Firm fixed effects ($\alpha_i$) capture persistent differences between firms. Quarter fixed effects ($\lambda_t$) capture shocks shared across firms in a quarter, and $\varepsilon_{it}$ is the error term. Standard errors would be clustered by firm because observations from the same firm may be correlated over time.

$\mathrm{Post}_t$ equals one from 2024Q4 onwards and zero before Helene. The individual exposure and post-event dummies are absorbed by the fixed effects. The coefficient $\beta$ measures the change in exposed firms relative to unexposed firms, in percentage points of pre-storm assets.

For R\&D, I would test $H_0: \beta \geq 0$ against $H_1: \beta < 0$. The complementary cash test is $H_0: \beta \leq 0$ against $H_1: \beta > 0$. I would use one-sided t-tests and report 95\% confidence intervals. The interpretation assumes that both groups would have followed parallel trends without Helene. Plots of pre-storm group means would help assess this assumption. A fall in R\&D alongside higher cash would fit the proposed liquidity response, while leaving its underlying motive unresolved.
