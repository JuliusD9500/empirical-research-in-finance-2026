\section*{Data}

% Dataset, coverage and source
The main analysis uses the simulated annual panel of German listed firms supplied through Moodle for this assignment. The file \nolinkurl{value_effect_panel.dta} contains 3,060 firm-year observations for 255 firms from 2010 to 2021, linking characteristics in year \(t\) to excess returns in \(t+1\). This panel allows us to examine the value effect across firms and over time. The accompanying \nolinkurl{cars_summary.dta} is used later for the Brexit event study and provides group-level abnormal returns and standard errors.

% Sample selection
We clean the main panel by excluding financial firms (two-digit SIC 60--69), whose balance sheets and leverage differ from those of non-financial firms. We also require complete identifiers and regression inputs, meaning that the firm and year are known and none of the values needed to estimate the models is missing. The final sample contains 2,664 observations for 230 firms. It is an unbalanced panel because some firms do not have an observation in every year. % Treatment of extreme values
To limit the influence of extreme values, we winsorise excess returns, book-to-market, leverage, profitability and dividend yield at 1\% in each tail across the final sample. This replaces the most extreme values with the respective boundary values without removing observations. Market capitalisation retains its original values to preserve the plausible size distribution. Its natural logarithm, our size measure, already compresses large differences and receives no further tail adjustment. % Economic interpretation of the observed ranges
Annual excess returns average 15.683\% and range from $-\,44.288\%$ to 74.637\%, a wide but economically possible range for individual stocks. Book-to-market ranges from 0.100 to 2.898, covering valuations above and below book equity. Leverage of 0.000--67.186\%, profitability of $-\,10.608\%$ to 25.816\%, and dividend yields of 0.000--5.520\% are plausible ranges that include loss-making and non-dividend-paying firms.

Market capitalisation ranges from EUR 0.407 million to EUR 24.491 billion. Its mean of EUR 324.475 million exceeds the EUR 44.917 million median, showing that a few very large firms pull the average well above the typical firm. This substantial right skew motivates the size controls used below. Log size ranges from $-\,0.899$ to 10.106. Negative log values simply indicate market capitalisation below EUR 1 million.
